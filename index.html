Quick context: the other two Reconstruction Amendments

13th Amendment — ratified Dec. 6, 1865
Abolished slavery and involuntary servitude, "except as a punishment for crime whereof the party shall have been duly convicted."
15th Amendment — ratified Feb. 3, 1870
Declared the right to vote could not be denied "on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude."

Document 2: The Black Codes (Mississippi & South Carolina, 1865)

Eight provisions from two of the first states to pass Black Codes after the war. Highlight the same way as above.

1. Mississippi, "Civil Rights of Freedmen" Act — November 1865. The law declared that freedpeople now had the legal right to marry, to own property, and to sue and be sued in court — rights they had been denied under slavery.

2. Mississippi, Vagrancy Law — November 1865. "All freedmen, free negroes and mulattoes... over the age of eighteen years, found... with no lawful employment or business... shall be deemed vagrants; and, on conviction thereof, shall be fined... and imprisoned at the discretion of the court."

3. Mississippi, Firearms Restriction — November 1865. "No freedman, free negro or mulatto, not in the military service of the United States government... shall keep or carry fire-arms of any kind, or any ammunition, dirk or bowie knife."

4. Mississippi, Intermarriage Ban — November 1865. "It shall not be lawful for any freedman, free negro or mulatto to intermarry with any white person... any person who shall so intermarry shall be deemed guilty of felony, and on conviction thereof, shall be confined in the State Penitentiary for life."

5. Mississippi, Freedman's Pauper Fund (poll tax) — November 1865. County police could levy an annual tax of up to one dollar on freedmen ages 18–60. Failure to pay counted as "prima facie evidence of vagrancy" — grounds for arrest and being hired out to pay off the debt.

6. South Carolina, Master and Servant Law — December 1865. "All persons of color who make contracts for service or labor, shall be known as servants, and those with whom they contract, shall be known as masters."

7. South Carolina, Occupation Restriction — December 1865. "No person of color shall pursue or practice the art, trade, or business of an artisan, mechanic, or shop-keeper, or any other trade... besides that of husbandry, or that of a servant under a contract for service or labor" without paying an annual license fee.

8. South Carolina, Migration/Bond Law — December 1865. "No person of color shall migrate into and reside in this State, unless... he shall enter into a bond, with two freeholders as sureties... in a penalty of one thousand dollars, conditioned for his good behavior." (That $1,000 bond, measured against average wages of the time, is roughly equivalent to $350,000 today.)
